## Reading

- **Warned judges do not get fooled; they abstain.** With the rubric sentence that an "ok" reply is not proof, no judge accepted more than 1 of 120 unverifiable failures. The price is coverage: they decline about 40–57% of cases, catch only about a third of false-success claims (Q3 recall), and confirm only 29%–67% of the episodes that genuinely completed. A warned transcript-only judge is safe but cannot certify outcomes. Addendum 1 tests whether the warning sentence is what does this.
- **A database snapshot does not make judges verifier-grade.** Gemma reaches 0.989; two families stay near 0.89. Majority voting (0.897) does not beat the best single judge.
- **Systematic bias found:** all four judges misread a refunded duplicate (payment, duplicate, refund rows: net one payment) as 'paid twice' — 4 cases where every judge contradicts the verifier, which is correct under the documented transaction semantics. Two-family errors concentrate on partial and duplicate cases.
- **Prompt sensitivity is large except for Gemma** (κ 0.96 vs 0.49–0.65).
