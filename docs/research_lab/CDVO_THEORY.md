# Counterfactual decision-value credit (CDVO): estimator analysis

The working hypothesis is that ActionProof's training methods credit *whether a read was rewarded*. They do not
credit *whether the read improved the value of the decisions that follow it*.

The candidate signal is the decision value of verification,

  Δ^π(h) = Q^π(h, verify) − Q^π(h, continue),  with Q^π(h, a) = E[G | h, do(a), π].

The return G includes completion utility, verification and recovery costs, duplicate and irreversible-operation
penalties, false-success penalties and truthful-reporting utility. In the testbed, G = V·succ − c·n_reads −
L_f·(1 − succ) − D·n_dup. The LLM's R_V and report terms are listed in `actionproof/stage3/`.

## 1. The paired fork estimator

At an on-policy decision point t with history h_t and full simulator state S_t = (h_t, z, ξ_{<t}):
1. Snapshot S_t.
2. Fork two copies, force a = verify in one and a = continue in the other.
3. Continue each copy with the **current** policy π_θ(· | h') on its *own* history h'. The continuation never
   replays the original trajectory's later actions.
4. Keep the latent fault plan z of S_t in both copies.
5. Couple exogenous randomness ξ_{≥t} (repair success, false alarms, later environment noise) by common random
   numbers.
6. Run each branch to termination, including recovery, irreversible operations and the final report, and record
   G_v and G_c.

The estimate is D̂ = G_v − G_c. The policy-gradient term at t is the all-action form Σ_a ∇π(a|h_t)·Q̂(h_t, a), with
Q̂ the fork returns. Equivalently it is ∇log π(a|h)·Â with the counterfactual advantage Â(h, a) = Q̂(h, a) − Σ_b π(b|h)Q̂(h, b).

**Implementation.** `actionproof/ad2/tabular/train.py:fork_return` (v2) and `actionproof/lab/cdvo.py`. Branches are
generated by `_op_ok`/`_op_timeout` from the forced action onwards with `pol.probs` on each branch's own history.
The actor's features never contain z (`tests/lab/test_cdvo.py::test_policy_inputs_never_contain_the_hidden_world`).

## 2. Unbiasedness (Proposition C1)

**Claim.** E[D̂ | h_t] = Δ^π(h_t) if the following hold:
- (i) S_t is the actual state of an on-policy trajectory, so z ~ P(z | h_t, π);
- (ii) both branches are continued by the same π for which Δ^π is wanted;
- (iii) the policy conditions on history only;
- (iv) the coupling of ξ preserves each branch's marginal distribution.

**Proof.**
1. By (iv), E[G_a | S_t] = Q^π(h_t, z, a), the history-state value of Baisero and Amato (AAMAS 2022). The coupling
   changes the joint law of (G_v, G_c), not the marginals.
2. By (i), E_z[Q^π(h_t, z, a) | h_t] = Q^π(h_t, a).
3. Linearity gives the claim. Unbiasedness for any K ≥ 1 forks follows. With the all-action form, the
   policy-gradient term is unbiased for Σ_a ∇π(a|h_t)·Q^π(h_t, a). Summed over on-policy decisions, this is the
   policy gradient (Sutton et al. 1999; the mean actor-critic of Allen et al. 2017).

Tested: `test_paired_fork_contrast_is_unbiased_for_the_current_policy`. The mean of 6,000 fork contrasts at the root
decision matches the exact Q^π difference from the expectimax walker, with and without CRN.

**What breaks it.**
- **(a) Replaying the original future.** If the original action sequence is replayed on the branch whose
  observations differ, the continuation is not π's. Q is then evaluated for an inconsistent policy, and the bias is
  arbitrary. The first-stage AWRB did not do this: it integrates over observation-consistent worlds with the
  *recorded* trajectory and needs trajectory consistency.
- **(b) Resampling z from a prior** instead of keeping the actual state gives unbiased Q only if the prior is the
  true posterior. v2's `fork_bel` failed under prior misspecification (TB4: regret 0.256 under ×0.25).
- **(c) A state-only critic Q(z, a)** in place of Q(h, z, a) is biased under partial observability (Baisero and
  Amato). The fork targets are history-state samples and do not have this problem.

## 3. Common random numbers (Proposition C2)

Var(D̂) = Var(G_v) + Var(G_c) − 2·Cov(G_v, G_c). CRN reduces variance iff the covariance is positive. Decompose
by the law of total variance over z:

  Var(D̂ | h) = E_z[Var(D̂ | z, h)] + Var_z(Δ^π(h, z)).

- CRN acts only on the first term, through ξ. The second term, the spread of the decision value across hidden
  states, is untouched by any coupling. One fork per state cannot remove it; K forks at the same snapshot cannot
  either, because they share z. Only more decision points, or belief-resampled forks, average over z.
- In a verify-vs-continue fork the two branches' **observation histories differ immediately**: the read result
  enters h'. Later policy draws are coupled only through shared uniforms, and the shared stream de-synchronises once
  the branches make different numbers of draws. Positive covariance comes mainly from shared exogenous events, such
  as later faults and repair noise.
- Prediction: CRN helps, but less than in fully observed control. In verification problems the dominant variance is
  often Var_z(Δ), because faulted and clean worlds have opposite-sign Δ. LAB1 H3 measures this.

## 4. Policy drift (Proposition C3)

A fork target computed under π_k but used to update π_{k+m} is biased by Q^{π_k} − Q^{π_{k+m}}. By the performance
difference lemma, for horizon H and |G| ≤ G_max, this is bounded by 2·H·G_max·max_h TV(π_k(·|h), π_{k+m}(·|h)).

Fresh forks at each step (`fork_act`, `cdvo_*`) have m = 0. A learned critic that stores past fork means
(`cdvo_adapt_critic`, EMA with weight 0.2) has m > 0. Its bias is largest exactly where the policy is changing
fastest.

## 5. Finite rollouts and stochastic continuations

With K forks, Var(Q̂) ∝ 1/K. The estimator stays unbiased (§2), but two caveats apply:
- With K = 1 the sign of D̂ is wrong with non-trivial probability whenever |Δ| is small relative to the
  continuation noise. A rank- or sign-based use of D̂ would be biased. The all-action gradient uses its value and
  is not.
- Forks are partial episodes. Their cost is charged pro rata ((K − j)/K of an episode, v2 convention). In an LLM the
  cost is generation tokens of the continuation, so the ratio differs. That has not been measured.

## 6. Can it be trained without an exact simulator?

The estimator needs a **resettable** environment: snapshot S_t, restore it twice, and run both continuations.
- **Training.** ActionProof's ProcureSim is a deterministic SQLite simulator, so snapshots are cheap. This is
  privileged training-time access; deployment needs nothing.
- **Real APIs.** Without restoration (real payment APIs), the paired contrast is unavailable. The alternatives:
  - a learned world model, biased by model error;
  - importance-weighted off-policy evaluation, high variance with long horizons;
  - sequence-level credit, such as Dr. GRPO.

  CRR (arXiv 2609.33875) states the same limitation: "applies to environments with affordable, reliable state
  restoration".

## 7. Comparison with COMA and actor-critic methods

| Method | Credit at decision t | Q source | Bias | Extra cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| REINFORCE / Dr. GRPO | (R − b)·∇log π(a_t\|h_t), with the same R for every decision of the trajectory | return | unbiased | none |
| PPO / A2C with V(h) | (G_t − V̂(h_t))·∇log π | learned V | critic error in the baseline only (unbiased for exact baselines) | critic |
| Mean actor-critic / EPG | Σ_a ∇π(a\|h)·Q̂(h, a) | learned Q | critic error | critic |
| COMA (Foerster et al. 2018) | Q̂(s, u) − Σ_{u'} π(u'\|τ)·Q̂(s, (u'^a, u^{−a})): a counterfactual baseline over the agent's own action, other agents fixed | learned centralised Q | critic error; state critic | critic |
| TRPO vine (Schulman et al. 2015) | Q̂(s_n, a_k) by K rollouts per action from rollout-set states, **CRN** | Monte Carlo | unbiased | K·A rollouts per state |
| VinePPO (arXiv 2410.01679) | Monte Carlo V(s_t) from re-sampled continuations | Monte Carlo | unbiased | rollouts |
| CRR (arXiv 2609.33875) | realised return − sampled counterfactual return at selected decision points | Monte Carlo fork | unbiased for the pairwise contrast | forks |
| **CDVO-full (= v2 `fork_act`)** | COMA's single-agent counterfactual advantage with **Monte Carlo** Q from paired forks with CRN | Monte Carlo fork | unbiased (§2) | forks at every verification decision |

With one agent, COMA's counterfactual advantage over the agent's own action is exactly the all-action advantage
above. CDVO replaces COMA's learned critic with TRPO-vine Monte Carlo estimates. **The estimator is therefore a
standard action-value contrast.** The two prior pieces are COMA's counterfactual advantage and TRPO's vine with CRN.
Their combination at selected agent decision points is CRR. Under the author's rule ("a standard action-value
estimator with a new name is not novel"), CDVO is not novel. The only application-specific element is the choice of
decision points: verification actions after aliased acknowledgements.

## 8. Selective computation (a separate hypothesis)

**Allocation rule tested** (`cdvo_adapt`). It spends the same expected fork count as `cdvo_fixed`, on the decisions
whose observable context has the smallest |mean Δ̂| / se, using forks from earlier steps only.
- Prior art: RSPI (Dimitrakakis and Lagoudakis 2008) allocates rollouts to states until a dominating action is
  identified (Hoeffding test, counting, UCB, successive elimination).
- EPIG-Tree (arXiv 2609.20004) derives the compute-optimal law n_e ∝ w_e·‖∇log π‖·σ_e/√c_e.
- ARPO (arXiv 2507.19849) branches at high-entropy steps after tool calls.

**Theoretical expectation.**
- Allocation by |Δ̂|/se puts forks where the *sign* of Δ is uncertain.
- The gradient contribution of a decision is π(1 − π)·Δ. Contexts with Δ ≈ 0 contribute little gradient whichever
  sign is right, and contexts with large |Δ| are already handled correctly by sequence-level credit (v2 V-F6).
- EPIG's law weights by the score norm and the value std, not by sign uncertainty, and is the better-founded rule.
  We test the RSPI-style rule because it is the hypothesis as posed. We record EPIG's law as the alternative we did
  not test.

**Prediction recorded before LAB1.** Adaptive allocation does not rescue forks at matched budget (H1 not supported).
The argument is that the decisions where forks change the update are uninformative reads, whose value −c is known
exactly (igc), and near-zero-Δ reads, where the stakes are small.
