\title{When Group Rollouts Share a Hidden State:\\Group Normalisation Cannot See the Stakes of Verification}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{abstract}
In agentic RL, the rollouts of a GRPO group usually start from the same task instance, so they share its hidden
environment state: here, which writes failed silently behind an ``ok'' acknowledgement. We show what this does to
learning a costly verification action.
\begin{itemize}
\item Under a shared state, GRPO's expected update is invariant to any per-state positive rescaling of the return.
A rare catastrophic fault therefore weighs no more than a common trivial one.
\item For a decision whose payoff is deterministic given the state, the update reduces exactly, at finite group size,
to a probability-weighted vote of signs. Std-free centring (Dr.~GRPO, RLOO) follows the on-policy action-value gap
under \emph{every} group law.
\item We give the exact scope of the resulting rule ``verify iff $p\,s>1/2$'': it holds only when repairability is
part of the shared state.
\item Independent resampling of states does not cure the bias. Reads that remove the state make groups homogeneous
again, so GRPO stalls at an interior read rate with $\Theta(1/G)$ regret.
\item Pairwise state correlation does not determine the update.
\end{itemize}
We ran a registered held-out testbed of 731 random problems, trained with an independently implemented sampler.
The exact theory predicts GRPO's learned decision in 721 of 721 decided cells, with median error 0.0003 in the
learned read probability. GRPO follows the vote, not the value, in 77 of 77 cells where the two disagree. In a 3.35B tool agent, all 800 logged groups per run share their fault plan. Re-weighting fixed
rollouts at a 20$\times$ higher read cost changes GRPO's clean-group update by 7\%. Every std-normalised run moves in
the direction of the vote estimated from its first 20 steps. In the registered five-seed comparison on 300 unseen tasks, Dr.~GRPO beats GRPO by $+0.120$ verified completion
(Holm $p=0.016$); every GRPO seed reads zero times, and all seven pre-stated theory predictions hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A payment API answers ``ok'', and the payment may or may not have been stored. A tool agent can
read the record back at a cost. Whether to read is a value-of-information decision: its worth grows with the
probability of a silent fault and with what an unrepaired fault costs.

Group-relative estimators standardise each rollout's return within its group~\cite{liu2025drgrpo,bay2026identity}.
Prior analyses take the group to be a prompt and the reward to be correctness. Under that reading, std normalisation
re-weights prompts by difficulty~\cite{liu2025drgrpo}, miscalibrates stochastic outcomes~\cite{bereket2025uncalibrated},
and optimises a monotone transform of per-prompt success~\cite{davis2025objective}.

In agent training the group key is larger: it is the prompt \emph{and} the hidden environment state attached to the
task instance. All $G$ rollouts of a task see the same database, repository or fault plan. We ask:
\emph{does group normalisation distort the learning of information acquisition when rollouts share a hidden state?}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(i) Per-state scale invariance.} Under a shared state, GRPO's expected update is invariant to per-state
positive affine maps of the return, for any policy and reward (Thm.~\ref{thm:inv}). Stakes across hidden states
cannot reach the update.
\item \textbf{(ii) The exact vote.} For one binary decision this gives a finite-$G$ vote. We delimit the folk rule
$p\,s>1/2$ and its ``cost-blindness'' exactly.
\item \textbf{(iii) A theory of group laws.}
\begin{itemize}
\item a $\pi$-free Bernstein representation of the update;
\item centred estimators are law-invariant;
\item \emph{endogenous homogeneity}: decorrelating states cannot remove the vote near always-verify;
\item the critical correlation depends on the law's family, not on pairwise correlation alone.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{(iv) Validation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item a registered held-out testbed;
\item mechanism diagnostics on a trained 3.35B agent;
\item theory predictions for a registered five-seed comparison, frozen before its outcome.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
We propose no new algorithm. Every candidate fix we examined is either already Dr.~GRPO or provably partial
(\S\ref{sec:disc}).

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Hidden state and decision.} A task has a hidden state $z\sim w$, e.g.\ clean, or a phantom, partial or
duplicated write. After an ``ok'' acknowledgement, which is identical in every $z$, the agent chooses $r\in\{0,1\}$
(continue or verify) with $\pi=\pi_\theta(r{=}1\mid h)$; this is the aliasing assumption A1. In the canonical
problem the state is faulted with probability $p$. A detected fault is repaired with probability $s$, success is
worth $V$, an unrepaired fault costs $L$, and a read costs $c$. Let $\beta_z=\mathbb E[R\mid z,1]-\mathbb E[R\mid z,0]$
and $\Delta Q=\sum_zw(z)\beta_z$. $\Delta Q$ equals net value of information only if the behaviour after the read is
Bayes-optimal; in general it is the on-policy action-value gap.

\paragraph{Groups and estimators.} A group is $G$ rollouts whose states follow an exchangeable law with marginal $w$
(A3), conditionally independent given the states (A2). Laws: \emph{shared}; \emph{iid}; \emph{mix}$(\rho)$ (shared
with probability $\rho$); \emph{dirmult}$(\rho)$ (Dirichlet-multinomial). The last two both have pairwise
correlation $\rho$. GRPO uses $A_i=(R_i-\bar R)/(\hat\sigma+\epsilon)$ with Bessel $\hat\sigma$ and
$\epsilon=10^{-4}$ (TRL); Dr.~GRPO uses $R_i-\bar R$ (our trained ``Dr.~GRPO'' arm takes this advantage but keeps TRL's
token-level aggregation; one update per batch, so the PPO clip is inactive; App.~\ref{app3:impl}). The expected update on the verify logit is
$U(\pi)=\mathbb E\sum_iA_i(r_i-\pi)$.

\paragraph{ActionProof.} ProcureSim tasks (order, receive, pay, post) carry one fault plan drawn from a fixed prior
(60\% clean). The reward is the verifier's $R_V\in\{0,1\}$ minus $0.05$ per read. Training: Tiny Aya Global (3.35B),
LoRA, TRL GRPO with 4 prompts $\times$ 8 rollouts and 200 steps. The fault plan is fixed per task, so \emph{every
group shares its hidden state}: we checked the code and 800 of 800 logged groups per run
(Appendix~\ref{app3:impl}). The only environment randomness is hashed ids, so within-group variation comes from the
policy.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
\begin{theorem}[per-state scale invariance]\label{thm:inv}
Under the shared law and $\epsilon\to0$, GRPO's expected update is unchanged by $R\mapsto\lambda_zR+\kappa_z$ for any
$\lambda_z>0$, $\kappa_z$, for any policy class and trajectory structure. Dr.~GRPO's update becomes
$(G-1)\sum_zw(z)\lambda_z\nabla J_z$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} A shared-state group's returns all come from $z$, and z-scoring is affine-invariant.
$\square$

\begin{corollary}[the vote; exact at finite $G$]\label{cor:vote}
If returns are deterministic within a state, $R=\alpha_z+\beta_zr$, then
$U_{\rm GRPO}=(G-1)m_G(\pi)\,\mathcal V$ with $\mathcal V=\sum_zw(z)\sgn\beta_z$ and
$m_G=\mathbb E[\sqrt{k(G-k)/(G(G-1))};0<k<G]$, $k\sim{\rm Bin}(G,\pi)$. The sign of $U$ is $\sgn\mathcal V$ at every
$\pi$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Scope of ``verify iff $p\,s>1/2$''.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{When it is exact.} If repairability is part of the state (repairable, unrepairable, clean) and
$0<c<V+L$, then $\mathcal V=2ps-1$: GRPO's update is positive iff $ps>1/2$, and identically zero at equality, whatever
$c$, $V$ or $L$.
\item \textbf{The $\epsilon$ boundary.} The exact threshold is $0.499$--$0.5005$ for $0.05\le c\le0.99$, but $0.452$
at $c=10^{-3}$.
\item \textbf{When it fails.} If repair success is rollout randomness, which is the LLM case, within-state returns are
not deterministic and the threshold depends on $c$: $0.654$, $0.667$, $0.706$ at $s=0.6$ and $c=0.01$, $0.05$, $0.2$,
against $1/(2s)=0.833$. Within-state noise from another source can make GRPO cost-sensitive again: with exogenous
failure $q_0=0.3$ it reaches the Bayes action (Appendix~\ref{app3:counter}).
\end{itemize}
Severity cannot enter. With two fault classes, multiplying the rare class's stakes by 1000 leaves GRPO's update
unchanged ($-2.961$) while Dr.~GRPO's turns from $-0.015$ to $+57.3$. All numbers are from exact enumeration
(\path{results/theory/scan.json}).

\begin{proposition}[Bernstein form; law-invariance of centring]\label{prop:bern}
For any centred estimator, $\sum_iA_i(r_i-\pi)=\sum_{i:r_i=1}A_i$, so
$U(\pi)=\sum_{k}C(G,k)\pi^k(1-\pi)^{G-k}b_k$ with $b_k$ independent of $\pi$ and $b_0=b_G=0$. Hence
$U'(0)=Gb_1$, $U'(1)=-Gb_{G-1}$, and $U$ has at most as many interior zeros as $(b_k)$ has sign changes. For
Dr.~GRPO, $b_k=\tfrac{k(G-k)}{G}\Delta Q$ and $U=(G-1)\pi(1-\pi)\Delta Q$ \emph{under every exchangeable law}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[endogenous homogeneity]\label{cor:homog}
If a read makes the return state-independent and skip returns are deterministic within a state, then under every
exchangeable law $U'(1)=-(G-1)\sqrt G\,\mathcal V$. For $\mathcal V<0<\Delta Q$ (e.g.\ $p<1/2$ with cheap, valuable
reads), always-verify is unstable whatever the sampler does to decorrelate states.
\end{corollary}
\noindent Near always-verify the readers' returns no longer depend on the state, so each lone skipper is
standardised to $\pm(G-1)/\sqrt G$ and the vote returns.

Under iid states GRPO therefore stalls at an interior $\pi^\ast$: $0.597$ at $p=0.3$, $c=0.05$, $G=8$, with regret
$0.101$. $G(1-\pi^\ast)$ converges as $G$ grows, to about $2.4$ at $p=0.3$, so the regret is $\Theta(1/G)$ rather than
$\Theta(1)$. For rare faults ($p\le0.1$) never-verify stays stable up to $G=32$ even with iid states.

\paragraph{Pairwise correlation is not enough.} mix$(\rho)$ is linear in $\rho$:
$U=\rho U_{\rm sh}+(1-\rho)U_{\rm iid}$. Never-verify becomes stable above
$\rho^\ast=b_1^{\rm iid}/(b_1^{\rm iid}-b_1^{\rm sh})$, which decays like $G^{-1/2}$ (0.211 at $G=8$, 0.149 at $G=64$,
$p=0.3$). dirmult$(\rho)$ has the same pairwise correlation, but its $\rho^\ast$ \emph{rises} towards a constant
(0.152 to 0.305). What matters is the probability that a group is payoff-homogeneous, not the correlation.

\paragraph{Other estimators.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zero-variance filtering} (DAPO) rescales $U$ without changing its sign.
\item \textbf{Per-component normalisation} of utility and cost (GDPO-style) never verifies under a shared state.
\item \textbf{Observation-measurable returns} (AWRB) recover $\sgn\Delta Q$ only for perfect detection and repair
and state-independent costs.
\end{itemize}
Proofs and 25 classified counterexamples are in Appendices~\ref{app3:proofs}--\ref{app3:counter}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp3}
\paragraph{Held-out testbed (TH1, registered).} We drew 600 random verification problems plus Dirichlet-multinomial
twins of every mixture cell. Their parameters:
\begin{itemize}
\item 1--3 fault states, severities up to 30, repairability, detection, false alarms with duplicate penalties;
\item state-dependent and log-uniform costs, exogenous noise;
\item $G\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, all four laws, four initial policies.
\end{itemize}
Ten estimators were trained with an implementation that shares no code with the theory's derivation (plain SGD, 300
steps, 16 groups per step, 10 seeds). Predictions come from the exact mean-field map. The registration was frozen
before any held-out output; three development runs fixed only the strata.

Five of seven registered hypotheses are supported (Table~\ref{tab:th1}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accuracy.} The mean-field map predicts the trained read probability with median error $0.0003$ (90th
percentile $\le0.0073$) for GRPO, Dr.~GRPO and RLOO. It predicts GRPO's decision in 721/721 cells.
\item \textbf{The vote.} In exact shared-state cells GRPO moves with the vote in 152/152. These include all 77 cells
where the vote and $\Delta Q$ disagree.
\item \textbf{Endogenous homogeneity.} With independent or partially shared states and state-neutralising reads,
GRPO stays below $\pi=0.95$ in 67/69 cells.
\item \textbf{Centring.} Dr.~GRPO follows $\sgn\Delta Q$ in 661/661 cells under every law.
\end{itemize}

Two hypotheses are not supported:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H6, not supported} (53/75). It asked that GRPO be no worse when the vote is right. In all 22 failures
GRPO reached the right corner, but slowly: its step size does not grow with the stakes. That is again
Thm.~\ref{thm:inv}, and the map predicted these $\pi_T$ to within 0.045.
\item \textbf{H7, untestable.} Only 3 twin pairs had a predicted difference $\ge0.05$ (all 3 in the predicted
direction).
\end{itemize}

Descriptively:
\begin{itemize}
\item zero-variance filtering (DAPO) lands where GRPO does (720/721);
\item PPO and batch-std follow $\sgn\Delta Q$ (0.970, 0.979);
\item per-component normalisation is worst (Bayes agreement 0.222).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanism in the trained agent (development, descriptive).} We recomputed both advantages on the
\emph{same} logged rollouts of 18 training runs (Appendix~\ref{app3:mech}).
\begin{itemize}
\item In GRPO runs, 111/146 to 127/163 of clean non-degenerate groups vary only through reads, the cost-only states
of Cor.~\ref{cor:vote}.
\item \textbf{Cost re-weighting.} Re-scoring the same rollouts at read cost 1.0 instead of 0.05 changes GRPO's
clean-group update by 7\% ($-898\to-962$); the centred update grows from $-86$ to $-1163$. This is
Thm.~\ref{thm:inv} on real data.
\item \textbf{Direction.} All 7 std-normalised runs move in the direction of the vote estimated from their first 20
steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item GRPO: vote $-0.375$ to $-0.583$, read share $0.19$--$0.22\to0.02$--$0.04$;
\item AWRB: $+0.56/+0.64$, $\to0.76/0.79$;
\item AWRB at $c=0.30$: $-1.0$, $\to0.02$.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{A negative result.} Dr.~GRPO's rise in reading ($0.23/0.28\to0.48/0.50$) is \emph{not} predicted by
the warm-start $\Delta Q$ (estimate $\le0$ or interval containing 0 in all 10 centred runs). $\Delta Q$ turns
positive later (last window $+0.02$ to $+0.11$, all 10 runs), as repair behaviour improves. The vote stays negative
throughout.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Registered five-seed comparison (AD-C1).} The registration (VSC on 300 unseen tasks, Dr.~GRPO vs GRPO,
permutation test, Holm) is unchanged. The theory's predictions were frozen before any outcome: supported direction,
difference in $[+0.05,+0.20]$; GRPO $\le0.2$ reads per episode in every seed; the gain located in faulted tasks; AWRB
over-verifies. Outcome (\path{results/ad/c1_summary.json}, confirmatory):
\begin{itemize}
\item H$_{\rm nostd}$ is supported: $+0.120$ [0.088, 0.153], Holm 0.016, positive in 5/5 seeds.
\item All five GRPO seeds read 0 times and score 0.687; Dr.~GRPO reads 0.93--2.02 times per episode.
\item The whole gain is on faulted tasks: clean-task difference 0.000, and faulted-task VSC 0.223 vs 0.521 accounts for
all of $+0.120$. The false-success gap is $0.122$.
\item AWRB reads more than Dr.~GRPO in 5/5 pairs (2.7 reads) and scores $+0.192$ over GRPO. AWRB-centred vs
Dr.~GRPO is not supported ($-0.088$).
\end{itemize}
All seven testable predictions (T1--T7) are met. Two caveats: the GRPO seeds are behaviourally identical, and
cost-blindness itself was not tested on the language agent.

\paragraph{Context: what else is known about this agent.}
\begin{itemize}
\item In the registered practical study (P1, 450 frozen test tasks, three seeds), recovery SFT and recovery SFT plus
GRPO beat the baseline by $+0.259$ and $+0.306$ VSC. An inference-time reconciliation line did not ($-0.030$;
\path{results/p1/p1_summary.json}). The two recovery arms start from different checkpoints, so their difference does not isolate what GRPO adds.
\item \textbf{Judges.} With a database snapshot the best LLM judge agrees with the verifier at F1 $0.990$. Without
it, the policy-family judge accepts 70\% of failed executions (\path{results/j2/j2_summary.json}). No human labels
exist, so we use the verifier as ground truth.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{What to change.} When groups share environment state and the task contains costly information actions,
do not divide by a group-local std. Centre only (Dr.~GRPO, RLOO), or use a scale that does not depend on the group's
hidden states. Decorrelating states inside groups is not a fix (Cor.~\ref{cor:homog}). We examined four candidate
interventions:
\begin{itemize}
\item state decorrelation;
\item external scales;
\item observation-measurable returns;
\item stake weighting.
\end{itemize}
Each is either Dr.~GRPO in expectation, provably partial, or needs hidden-state labels. So we propose none.

\paragraph{Beyond verification.} Thm.~\ref{thm:inv} is about any decision whose consequences differ across hidden
states that a group shares. It predicts that GRPO-trained agents under-weight rare high-stakes states. Paired-noise
samplers~\cite{sakib2026luck} and fixed task snapshots create exactly this structure. A cost-insensitive collapse has
been reported for GRPO on costed exploration~\cite{ding2026calibrate}; its group construction is not reported, so we
do not claim it as an instance.

\section*{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item The exact results cover one binary decision. The multi-decision case is covered by Thm.~\ref{thm:inv}, which is
exact but not quantitative, and by experiments.
\item The LLM evidence is one model family, one simulator and development seeds, plus one registered comparison.
\item The warm-start $\Delta Q$ does not predict Dr.~GRPO's trajectory.
\item The read count includes uninformative reads.
\item No human-labelled judge evaluation exists.
\item Novelty is incremental: the invariance of z-scores within homogeneous groups is noted
by~\citet{wang2026darkroom} for all-fail groups. Our contribution is the hidden-state group key and its exact
decision-theoretic consequences.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
Synthetic purchase orders only. The mechanism can make agents skip checks whose stakes are high but whose failure
states are rare. We recommend scales independent of hidden state and deterministic verification of writes.
